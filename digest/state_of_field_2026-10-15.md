# State of the Field — 2026-10-15

**Scope caveat, stated up front.** This rests on a single 30-day seeding
backfill: 17 kept articles, 7 at score 4-5, 6 of the 17 preprints, and every
summary drawn from an abstract with no full text fetched
[2026-10-15 · cumulative header]. It is a **baseline**, not a trend. Nothing
below should be read as a direction of travel; it is a description of where this
month's evidence sits, written so that next month's evidence has something to
disagree with.

## The argument: loading has outrun measurement

The clearest thing in this month's set is that the field can now put far more
cargo on a targeting molecule than it can account for. A click-chemistry
polypeptide backbone reached DAR 61 with antigen binding and endocytosis
retained and subnanomolar cellular activity — roughly an order of magnitude past
the conventional DAR 2-8 window [cumulative §3 · PMID:42819270]. That is a real
capability. But the same abstract gives no PK parameters, no dosing and no
tumor-response numbers, so copies-per-antibody is never joined to
cargo-delivered-per-dose [cumulative §6]. The only paper in the set that joins
loading to a dose-matched in-vivo outcome does it at DAR 1 versus 2, where a
C16 lipid on the siRNA restored DAR2 potency and halved the antibody needed per
unit of siRNA delivered [cumulative §3 · DOI:10.64898/2026.09.23.753708,
preprint].

So the field's two loading results sit at opposite ends of a curve nobody has
drawn. One shows 61 copies are chemically attachable; the other shows that
going from 1 to 2 copies was enough to cost potency until a specific chemical
fix was found. Those two facts are hard to hold simultaneously without
concluding that **payload copies per antibody and payload delivered per dose
are different quantities, and only the first is being optimized.** That is the
gap worth owning.

The same asymmetry appears in transit. pIgR engagement moved IgG-format cargo
into a mucosal lumen in cynomolgus monkeys at 5-10-fold enrichment — a genuine
non-human-primate barrier-crossing result — but the measurement is lavage
fluid, not tissue, and no cargo was carried [cumulative §1 · PMID:42829333].
TfR1 VHH clones diverged strikingly in brain localization, neuronal and
sustained versus vascular and declining, with no %ID/g, no
parenchyma-to-capillary ratio and no cargo [cumulative §1, §6 ·
PMID:42855849]. Localization is being reported where delivery is the claim of
interest.

## What looks like it is consolidating

**The antibody is becoming a component rather than the vehicle.** A nanobody
clicked onto an LNP transfected up to 48% of *resting* primary human CD4+ T
cells [cumulative §5 · PMID:42764379]; two antibodies were co-displayed on one
LNP to address two cell types [cumulative §5 ·
DOI:10.64898/2026.09.21.753074, preprint]; an antibody was used purely as a
host-guest docking site for particles infused later [cumulative §5 ·
PMID:42839730]; a nanobody was built into an AAV capsid [cumulative §5 ·
DOI:10.64898/2026.09.18.752817, preprint]. In four of five carrier-format
papers the binder supplies specificity and something else supplies capacity.
That is a coherent answer to the cargo-per-targeting-unit problem, and it is
where the month's energy is.

**It also has a shared methodological weakness.** Every one of those papers is
missing the untargeted comparator number that would size the gain, and several
defer PK and biodistribution outright, in two cases by the authors' own
statement [cumulative §6]. The format is being demonstrated faster than it is
being quantified.

## What is stuck, and what nobody is doing

Two of the six themes are thin enough to be worth naming. **Valency and
affinity** produced no top pick; the nearest thing to a controlled valency
experiment is on an aptamer-bearing DNA framework with affinity unmatched
[cumulative §2 · PMID:42766830]. Nobody in this set separated valency from
affinity in an antibody or nanobody shuttle. **Endosomal escape and cytosolic
delivery were not measured by any paper in the backfill** [cumulative §4, §6] —
an absence, recorded as such. What occupies the trafficking theme instead is
degradation: three papers route a target to the lysosome and measure its
disappearance [cumulative §4 · PMID:42816619, PMID:42801692, PMID:42766830].
Those are good trafficking assays attached to the wrong endpoint for this
thesis, and the most useful thing in them may be the assays themselves.

One result deserves to stay uncomfortable. A cleavable ADC payload acted on
melanoma xenografts with little antibody internalization, apparently via
extracellular cathepsin L [cumulative §4 ·
DOI:10.64898/2026.10.09.757939, preprint]. If that generalizes for cleavable
small molecules, then the uptake-efficiency program and the loading program are
optimizing for different payload classes, and conflating them will waste
effort. Section 6 records what would settle it.

**The experiment nobody in this set ran:** a copy-number series on one binder
with cargo copies, retained affinity, uptake per cell, clearance and delivered
cargo reported together. Until someone does, "higher DAR" remains a chemistry
claim rather than a delivery claim.
